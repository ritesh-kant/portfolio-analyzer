BT45 — the cost-aware stop, trade by trade

Each card is one real trade replayed twice: once with the cost-aware stop and once without. The solid amber line is the cost stop — the price the stop is lifted to at 1R so that selling there covers the actual charges. The hollow grey marker is where the same trade exited without the rule. Support/resistance is computed as of the entry bar only. Costs are shown twice: bt17's stressed number and a realistic one recomputed per trade with the itemised MIS model. Descriptive report of an already-measured, already-killed rule — not a new test.

Read this before the charts. These 10 trades were hand-picked from the 184 (out of 3,139) whose exit the rule changed at all, chosen to show both what it saves and what it costs. Their combined P&L means nothing on its own. The measured answer across the whole 3,139-trade test is -0.09 ₹ per trade, p = 0.893 — indistinguishable from zero, and the rule was killed on that basis. What the charts are for is seeing why it nets out: the amber line banks a small win on trades that were going to scratch, and ends trades that were still going.
