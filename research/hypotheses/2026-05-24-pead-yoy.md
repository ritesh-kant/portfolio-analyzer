# Hypothesis: PEAD v3 — YoY EPS Growth Signal (No Consensus Dependency)

## 1. Hypothesis

When a Nifty Midcap 150 stock reports quarterly EPS at least 25% higher than the
same quarter last year (both quarters profitable), the stock drifts upward over
the following **5 trading days** by ≥ 100 bps net of costs, with per-trade
Sharpe ≥ 0.5.

## 2. Why v2 Failed — and Why v3 Fixes It

PEAD v2 (2026-05-20) was killed with 6 of 7 gate criteria failing. The root
cause was **consensus estimate data quality**, not the PEAD mechanism:

- v2 relied on screener.in-scraped consensus EPS to compute surprise magnitude.
- Scraped consensus data is unreliable: stale estimates, wrong quarter mapping,
  missing values, ToS risk.
- A signal built on unreliable inputs produces an unreliable backtest.

**PEAD v3 removes the consensus dependency entirely.**

The YoY comparison (this quarter vs same quarter last year) is:

1. Computed directly from NSE quarterly filings — already in the data lake with
   PIT discipline (`yoy_eps_prev` field in `nse_results.parquet`).
2. Self-contained: no third-party data, no scraping, no ToS risk.
3. Robust: both EPS values are audited, public, point-in-time correct.

## 3. Mechanism

### 3.1 Why YoY EPS growth ≥ 25% signals underreaction

A Nifty Midcap 150 stock has median analyst coverage of ~6 analysts (vs ~25 for
Nifty 50). When a lightly-covered stock grows earnings 25%+ YoY:

1. **Institutional under-reaction**: large funds process mid/small-cap earnings
   on a delay (days to weeks after announcement); their buying creates sustained
   post-announcement drift.
2. **Retail anchoring**: retail investors anchor to the prior year EPS; a 25%
   jump shifts their mental model slowly, not instantaneously on announcement day.
3. **Index fund forced buying**: if the stock approaches index threshold weights,
   passive funds must accumulate — amplifying continuation.
4. **No analyst revision lag** needed: unlike consensus-based PEAD, this signal
   fires immediately on the filing date, before analysts revise their models.

### 3.2 Why 5-day hold

Academic PEAD literature (Bernard & Thomas 1989; Jegadeesh & Livnat 2006) shows
the largest drift concentration in days 1–5 post-announcement. After day 5,
returns taper as fast institutional money has re-positioned. A 5-day hold:

- Captures the primary drift window
- Limits exposure to broader market noise (lower std than 20-day)
- Higher Sharpe than longer holds (mean doesn't decay as fast as std)

### 3.3 Why 25% threshold

The YoY EPS growth distribution across all Midcap 150 quarterly results
(2015–2024) has:

- Median growth: ~0.4% (near zero)
- 75th percentile: ~47%

A 25% threshold catches approximately the top 40% of growth quarters
among stocks with positive EPS in both periods — a meaningful positive
surprise for lightly-covered midcap stocks. Round numbers (10%, 20%, 25%,
30%) are all defensible; 25% was pre-selected as the midpoint of the range
that avoids both noise (< 20%) and scarcity (> 40%).

**This threshold is immutable — do not adjust after seeing dev results.**

## 5. Signal Construction (pre-registered, immutable)

```python
# On each business date T (earnings announcement date):
# 1. Load quarterly results announced on date T
# 2. Filter: symbol in Midcap150_members(T)
# 3. Filter: result_type == "quarterly"
# 4. Filter: eps_reported > 0 AND yoy_eps_prev > 0  (both quarters profitable)
# 5. Filter: (eps_reported - yoy_eps_prev) / yoy_eps_prev >= 0.25  (≥25% growth)
# 6. Election filter: skip events within ±30 calendar days of Lok Sabha election
# 7. Pledge filter: skip stocks flagged by promoter pledge filter
# 8. Aggregate multiple same-day results for same symbol: one event per (symbol, date)
# 9. Entry: T+1 open price
# 10. Exit: T+5 close price
# 11. Net return: (exit / entry - 1) - 0.0055
```

**No momentum filter** — PEAD is an earnings-driven mechanism. EMA50
filter was appropriate for block deals (trend continuation); for earnings
drift, a stock below its EMA50 can still have a sharp upside reaction to
a positive earnings surprise.

## 6. Cost Assumption

Round-trip cost: 55 bps (same as all prior strategies). With a 5-day hold
and expected mean ~150–250 bps, costs are ~20–35% of gross return. The
cost-stress gate tests whether DSR survives 2× slippage stress.

**5/7 pass, 5 criteria FAIL → KILLED**

**Post-mortem:**

Both long (−40.6 bps) and short (−69.4 bps) directions lose money. This
means implied gross return ≈ +14 bps — barely above zero. After 55 bps
round-trip costs, both directions are net negative. The signal has no
predictive power.

**Diagnosis: "buy the rumor, sell the news"**

YoY EPS ≥ 25% growth in a Midcap 150 stock is already partially anticipated
by the T+1 open. Institutional investors and algo traders scan NSE filings
at 18:00 IST; by the next morning open, the good-earnings trade is crowded.
The T+1 open price incorporates the full reaction; the next 5 days see mean
reversion, not continuation.

This is the opposite of PEAD — the announcement effect is front-run by
sophisticated players so completely that there is no residual drift to capture
with a rules-based entry at T+1 open.

**Election filter distortion:** 79 of 266 events (30%) were removed by the
election filter. The dev period (2023-07 → 2024-06) covers the full 2024
Indian election cycle, which coincides with Q4 results season. Even without
the election filter, the gross return would need to be ~300 bps to overcome
costs — there is no evidence of that.

## 9. Decision

**KILLED — 2026-05-24.** 5 of 7 gate criteria failed.

**Full rules-based strategy audit (A–L):**

Every rules-based approach in the plan has now been tried and killed.
The closest result was Strategy I (block deals, Sharpe 0.389), which
demonstrated real edge but insufficient Sharpe.

**Conclusion:** Rules-based signals alone cannot clear the Sharpe ≥ 0.5 gate
on Indian midcap data. The mechanism exists (PEAD is academically validated;
block deals show genuine win rate) but per-trade variance is too high for
a simple rules filter to produce Sharpe ≥ 0.5.

**Required next step:** The original plan (§5.1, Month 3) always intended a
**ML-based PEAD** — LightGBM combining multiple features to predict _which_
PEAD events will drift up. Rules-based was exploration. The correct path is:

1. Feature engineering on reliable, in-house data:
   - EPS YoY growth % (already computed)
   - Revenue YoY growth %
   - Day-0 price reaction (open-to-close on announcement day)
   - Day-0 volume vs 20-day average
   - Pre-announcement momentum (EMA50 filter)
   - Net profit margin trend (3-quarter moving average)
2. LightGBM classifier predicting P(5-day return > 100 bps)
3. Trade only events with P > threshold
4. This is Strategy M — pre-register before implementing.
